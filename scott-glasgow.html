The origin story

FinMango
A 501(c)(3) since 2016.

The spark came from class discussions at Kent State University, led by two professors, Dr. Via and Dr. Thornton. Ideas come and go. This one felt different.

My passion for finance and, yes, mangoes led to FinMango: an energy bar company with a charitable foundation attached. The business failed. The foundation thrived. Sometimes the thing you didn't plan is the thing that works.

On a trip to San Francisco with my friend Oscar, we reimagined FinMango as a force for financial health worldwide. With Steffan Dwarkasingh and Jeff Jensen we built the nonprofit and launched our first program in Canton, Ohio. The Money Cycle curriculum reached 13 countries soon after.

Ten years on, our research and data inform the WHO, World Bank, and IMF, and the mission hasn't changed: financial health is a right, not a privilege.

Deep cuts

Things that aren't on my LinkedIn.

🛶 01

Team meetings on kayaks.

The early team met on the water and worked late nights at Starbucks. Office space was optional. Momentum wasn't.

🎤 02

One talk, 50,000 students.

Speaking to 500 students at University of Akron Finance Day, I met Bob Gillingham. As "Mango Bob," his investing class went on to reach 50,000+ students.

🎓 03

15 minutes with Steve Ballmer.

A chance conversation on Harvard's campus with teammate Brandyn Neal that doubled down our commitment to the mission.

⏱️ 04

A 24-hour world record on Zoom.

EXTRAVAGANZA, 2020: 24 straight hours of live content, 1,600+ attendees, and 300,000+ people reached on social.

📰 05

An op-ed that trended.

Co-written with Oscar Wahltinez on COVID-19 data for K-12 schools, then picked up by CNN, The New York Times, Fortune, and NBC.

🧪 06

A "semi-secret moonshot factory."

Mango X, founded in 2021, builds tools and calculators now used by thousands of teachers nationwide.
