Explainer · Food access
Illustrative scores worked from the zip code digits, not a data lookup

What makes a neighborhood a food desert?

Type a zip code and you get three scores: grocery access, fresh food and transportation. They are a teaching example, not a reading of your area. The scores are generated from the zip code itself, so the same zip always gives the same answer. The real data lives in the USDA Food Access Research Atlas, linked again below.

Five digits. Press Enter or the button, then try a neighbouring zip and see how little it takes to flip the result.

What the real measures look at

Food access researchers, including the USDA, look at three things. The scores above borrow the three names so you can see how they fit together, but only the atlas has the measurements for your actual tract.

Grocery store accessHow far people live from a supermarket or large grocery store. Low-income areas more than 1 mile (urban) or 10 miles (rural) from a store are counted as low access.
Fresh food availabilityWhether nearby stores sell fresh produce, lean proteins and whole grains. Areas served only by convenience stores or fast food score lower.
Transportation accessPublic transit and how many households have a vehicle. When neither is reliable, a store two miles away can be out of reach.

For official food desert data, use the USDA Food Access Research Atlas. It maps low-income, low-access census tracts from real store locations.
