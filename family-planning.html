Put in both salaries, any time one of you plans to spend out of work, childcare, your tax rates and what you save for retirement. Before you start

This simulator is a collaboration between FinMango and Modern Husbands. It helps two-income couples and families see where their money is likely to be in 15 years.

It takes in household income, time one of you might spend out of the workforce, childcare, healthcare and other living costs, taxes, and retirement savings.

Every field starts with an example number. Change them to match your own situation; the more of them you fill in, the closer the answer gets.

Household income

Family type

Annual salaries

Enter the annual salary for each spouse or partner, before tax.

Time away from work

Unpaid time either of you plans to spend out of the workforce, for a new child or anything else. The salary drops to zero for those years and restarts at its old level afterwards.

Childcare

Any extra childcare you will pay for, such as daycare, on top of the baseline cost of a child that is already built in. It is added for up to five years, starting the year after any career break ends.

Childcare prices by age of child, from the US Department of Labor

Living expenses

Your total monthly basic living costs today, including healthcare: housing, transportation, food, utilities and health costs.

What should I include?

Your current monthly spending on:

  • Housing (rent or mortgage)
  • Transportation (car payments, insurance, public transit)
  • Food
  • Utilities
  • Healthcare premiums and typical out-of-pocket costs

Leave out anything child-related. Those costs are counted separately.

Taxes

Rough rates are fine. These are applied as flat percentages to income after retirement contributions, so they will not match an exact tax return.

Growth and retirement

How you expect salaries and costs to move each year, and how much of each salary goes into retirement accounts. Retirement balances are grown at 8% a year.

Your 15-year projection

The projection runs 15 years and covers income, taxes, child costs, living expenses and retirement saving, with each of them growing at the rates you set.

The child cost in each year combines your extra childcare with the baseline cost of one child. The USDA's 2024 inflation-adjusted estimate for that baseline is around $18,000 a year.
