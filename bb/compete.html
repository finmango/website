How it works

What you'll do

01

Find your market

Pick a cost that keeps families from getting ahead. Work out who it hurts, what it costs them, and why nobody has fixed it. That's your customer research.

02

Build the venture

An app, a product, a service, a community program, a policy campaign. Decide how it reaches people and how it keeps running: who pays, who delivers, what it costs. Use AI tools. Prototype fast. Make it real.

03

Pitch the panel

Bring a poster, plus anything else that shows your idea: a prototype, a demo on your laptop, a 3D print. Two judges visit your table like investors working a floor. You pitch for 60 seconds, then take their questions.

The format

Every team is judged. Five reach the stage.

Every team gets the same morning and the same two judges as everyone else in its section.

Morning

Poster Gallery

The room is split into ten sections. A panel of two judges visits every table in its section: a 60-second pitch, a few minutes of questions, then scoring.

11:15 AM

Finalists named

Each panel names its section's top team. The five highest-scoring of those ten become the finalists. The other five are honorable mentions.

11:25 AM

Demo Day

Five finalists present to the whole room and a separate panel of five judges. Three-minute pitch, two minutes of questions each.

Why sections? With ten sections, two judges have time for a real conversation at every table, and everyone you're up against for your section's top spot pitched to the same two people. Sections are mixed by topic and school, so no panel is comparing six projects from one classroom.

Prepare to win

Three questions, 1 to 5 each.

From every judge who sees you. The highest panel total in your section goes to Demo Day.

Judging rubric & scorecard
1–5

Do they get the problem?

Who it costs, how much, and why it's stuck. A 5 can name the customer and the numbers.

1–5

Would it actually work?

Something on the table, a way it would run and keep running, and it reaches the people from question one.

1–5

Could they explain it?

Clear in 60 seconds, honest under questions, and every team member can speak to it. Have a three-minute version ready in case you're called up.

What to bring

  • A poster for your table. Hand-made is fine. Bring anything else that makes your idea real too: a prototype, a 3D print, an app on a laptop.
  • A rehearsed 60-second pitch. Judges keep time, and the stop is hard.
  • A three-minute presentation, ready to go. Finalists are named at 11:15 and present at 11:25, and nobody knows in advance, so every team sends its slides in by Friday, March 5.
  • Your phone, for the Knowledge Track quiz.

Good to know

  • Food is provided. Breakfast snacks and lunch for all attendees.
  • Parking is free in designated lots. Details are sent before the event.
  • Dress code: business casual recommended for presenters.
  • Guests are welcome. Family members, teachers, and non-competing students can attend.
