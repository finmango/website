## LinkedIn — document post (`finmango-the-best-investment-linkedin.pdf`)

"The most valuable investment I made wasn't financial. It was investing in my
ability to adapt."

Varsha Tulsani, a FinMango Ambassador, was laid off. For months afterward,
financial health meant one number to her: the balance in her bank account.

Then she changed the question. Instead of "how long will my savings last," she
started asking "what can I build while I'm here?"

Leadership programs. New skills. Volunteering. Writing every week. None of it
came with a paycheck. Some days none of it came with visible results either.
It rebuilt her confidence anyway.

She now works with students and young professionals who are not asking how to
earn more. They are asking where to begin. Their challenge isn't ambition.
It's access.

Which is the whole distinction we keep coming back to:

Financial literacy teaches us how money works.
Financial health asks whether people can navigate life with confidence,
resilience, and the support they need when circumstances change.
