## LinkedIn — document post (`finmango-financial-independence-young-women-linkedin.pdf`)

"One is not born, but rather becomes a woman," Simone de Beauvoir wrote.
Mariana Nunes Santos Gomes argues financial autonomy works the same way: not a
starting point, but something built through knowledge and opportunity.

She writes from Brazil, where young women, and especially Black women from
low-income and peripheral communities, face informality, low wages, and no
access to credit or seed capital. Where money is taboo at home and the market
is structured to exclude.

Her case is not only moral. She cites the World Bank: gender equality in the
labor market could add at least 20% to global GDP. Financially independent
women invest in their families, their communities, and their businesses.
