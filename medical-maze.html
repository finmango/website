Game · A medical emergency
Made-up scenario: $2,000 fund, $500 in-network copay, out-of-network bills of $3,500 and $2,800

Can you get through one emergency without debt?

You have insurance and a $2,000 emergency fund. It is Tuesday night and something is wrong. Make your choices and see what the bill looks like three weeks later. The numbers are invented to show how the pieces fit together, not to quote real prices.

Emergency fund$2,000
Three weeks later

It's Tuesday night. You feel a sharp pain in your side. It's getting worse. You have a $2,000 emergency fund and "good" insurance. What do you do?
How this is worked out

Every path ends with a bill. The hospital stay is in-network with a $500 copay, but the surgery path adds an out-of-network anesthesiologist and the ambulance path adds a private ambulance company, and those two are what you end up owing. Whatever the bill is, your $2,000 fund pays first and the rest is debt.

Why the bills add up
Surprise billing You can go to an in-network hospital and still be treated by an out-of-network doctor. You have no say over who is on call, but you get the bill.
Deductibles The average deductible is over $1,700. Most Americans do not have that in savings, so one emergency can wipe out years of saving.
Bankruptcy Medical bills are the leading cause of bankruptcy in the US. About 66.5% of bankruptcies are tied to medical issues.
