The premise

For a 25-year-old today, the affordability crisis isn't one bad month. It's rent that takes a third of the paycheck, a loan balance that barely moves, and a medical bill waiting to happen — compounding quietly for years. Our work is to make that slow squeeze measurable, and then impossible to ignore.

05 · Open questions

What we're asking.

A research agenda is really a list of honest questions — the quiet ones, the inconvenient ones, the ones the data keeps hinting at. These are the six we're chasing now. Open one to see why it matters and what it's already feeding.

01

Can search behavior predict an eviction before it's filed in court?

Why it matters

An eviction filing is a lagging indicator. By the time a case reaches court, the damage to credit, health and housing stability is already done. If searches for help spike weeks earlier, cities could put emergency rental assistance in front of the right households in time.

Where it's going
Working paper Housing Policy Lab Barometer
02

Does one medical event really erase a generation of savings — and for whom?

Why it matters

A single emergency-room visit can undo years of saving, and young adults are the group most likely to be uninsured or under-insured. We're mapping who absorbs the hit, where the bill lands, and what it does to everything else in the budget.

Where it's going
The Medical Maze Reports & briefs
03

How do community banks absorb the financial aftershock of a tornado?

Why it matters

When a tornado flattens a town, the local bank is often the only lender that knows it. We're studying how community banks absorb the shock, keep credit flowing, and what happens to households when they can't.

Where it's going
Working paper Disaster Desk
04

What does AI adoption in banking mean for the people behind the teller window?

Why it matters

Banks are automating fast. We're tracking what that means for entry-level jobs, the first rung on the ladder for many young adults, and for who gets approved by a model instead of a person.

Where it's going
Working paper AI & the Economic Signal
05

Where does financial literacy change behavior — and where is it just theater?

Why it matters

Everyone agrees it matters. Far fewer measure whether it changes behavior. We compare what people search for before and after they learn, so education gets built on evidence instead of good intentions.

Where it's going
Working paper Education
06

When does student debt stop compounding disadvantage, if ever?

Why it matters

For millions of young adults the loan payment competes directly with rent. We're measuring how that competition plays out state by state, and when, if ever, the balance stops compounding disadvantage.

Where it's going
Loan Payoff Simulator Young Adult Map
From signal to solution

The whole loop, in house.

Most research stops at the paper. Ours doesn't: the same team that spots the signal builds the tool and carries the evidence to the people who can act on it.

  1. 01

    Signal

    Search behavior, rents, wages and prices across all 50 states, refreshed as new data lands.

  2. 02

    Measure

    The Barometer turns those signals into a 0–200 stress index for every state, and a young-adult index on top.

  3. 03

    Explain

    Plain-language briefs and peer-reviewed papers. Every method and every limitation published.

  4. 04

    Build

    Free tools young adults actually use: loan simulators, rent vs. buy, the medical maze, a paycheck calculator.

  5. 05

    Change

    Evidence in front of the people who set policy, from WHO and the World Bank to city halls and campuses.

06 · Approach & methodology

Why we do what we do.

We identify the systemic barriers endangering young adults' financial health — housing, healthcare, debt, predatory lending. We diagnose root causes. We provide the evidence decision-makers need to build solutions that actually work.
