What you'll do

1

Find your market

Pick a cost that keeps families from getting ahead. Work out who it hurts, what it costs them, and why nobody has fixed it. That's your customer research.

2

Build the venture

An app, a product, a service, a community program, a policy campaign. Decide how it reaches people and how it keeps running: who pays, who delivers, what it costs.

3

Pitch the panel

Bring a poster, plus anything else that shows your idea: a prototype, a demo on your laptop, a 3D print. Two judges visit your table. You pitch for 60 seconds, then take their questions.

Pick a barrier

These are the costs we see hurting families most. Build around one, or bring your own. There are no tracks to sign up for. Every team competes in the same round.

  • TransportationCar costs, insurance, and transit gaps blocking stable employment.
  • Housing affordabilityRent burden, deposits, and credit barriers to stable housing.
  • Healthcare accessMedical debt and high costs devastating young adults.
  • Literacy and investmentNo one taught them to budget, save, or invest, so wealth never builds.
  • Your ownChildcare, food insecurity, student debt, wage theft, or a barrier only you've noticed.

Every team is judged. Five reach the stage.

Every team gets the same morning and the same two judges as everyone else in its section.

9:20 AM

Poster Gallery

The room is split into ten sections. Two judges visit every table in their section: a 60-second pitch, a few minutes of questions, then scoring.

11:15 AM

Finalists named

Each pair of judges names its section's top team. The five highest-scoring of those ten are the finalists. The other five are honorable mentions.

11:25 AM

Demo Day

The five finalists present to the whole room and a separate panel of five judges. Three minutes to pitch, two minutes of questions.

Why sections? Two judges have time for a real conversation at every table, and everyone you're up against for your section's top spot pitched to the same two people. Sections are mixed by topic and school.

How you're scored

Three questions, 1 to 5 each, from every judge who sees you. The highest total in your section goes to Demo Day. Read the full rubric (PDF)

1–5

Do they get the problem?

Who it costs, how much, and why it's stuck. A 5 can name the customer and the numbers.

1–5

Would it actually work?

Something on the table, a way it would run and keep running, and it reaches the people from question one.

1–5

Could they explain it?

Clear in 60 seconds, honest under questions, and every team member can speak to it.
