slide 08 / 11 — from the field
FinMango ·From the Field·07
Sayula, Jalisco · Mexico, 2024

Fifty women.
One month.
Almost no attrition.

Female Financial Autonomy ran three hours every Saturday plus Wednesday evenings online. Nearly all participants were mothers running their own businesses. By conventional expectations, attrition should have been high.

Almost all fifty finished.

Program funded by the U.S. Department of State →
slide 09 / 11 — what it revealed
FinMango ·What It Revealed·08

The binding constraint
was never information

Several participants had never approached a formal financial institution — not for lack of information, but because they expected to be rejected or treated poorly. The real barriers were trust, risk perception, networks, and norms about who is permitted to make economic decisions.

The program ended with participants pitching to a committee that included the U.S. Consul General in Guadalajara. Attendance and preparation rose sharply — because someone with institutional standing was going to take their business seriously.
